How I decide

Build what has a hard problem inside it. Buy what is risk without insight.

Every non-obvious decision in the DeepCS design doc carries one of three tags. The same test produces opposite answers in different places — and that consistency is the point.

[bought]

Deliberately not built. There's no interesting problem inside it, so paying for it is the correct engineering call, not a shortcut. Auth is bought — identity is security-critical and fully solved, and Google's key rotation beats anything I'd hand-roll.

[built · learning]

A mature off-the-shelf option exists and is what I'd use commercially. Built here because the problem underneath is one I wanted to hit directly. The gateway is built — Kong would fix its rate-limiter race on my behalf, and that race is the whole lesson.

[detour · learning]

Built, run, then deliberately not kept in production. Kubernetes is a detour — stood up on GKE beside the live deploy, demonstrated, then deleted, because an always-on cluster breaks the cost ceiling the whole design is shaped by.

The failure mode this doc is written to prevent isn't picking a wrong tool; it's spending three weeks on infrastructure and one on the only part that's hard. — DeepCS DESIGN.md, closing note
